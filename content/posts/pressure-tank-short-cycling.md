A pressure tank has one job: give the pump somewhere to push so it does not
start every time somebody runs a tap.

When the bladder fails the tank fills with water, the air cushion is gone, and
the pump starts and stops on a few seconds of draw. Nothing obviously breaks.
The water still runs. The customer notices nothing for months.

What it does is run a submersible pump through tens of thousands of starts it
was never rated for. Starting current is where the heat is. We have pulled
pumps at four years old that should have gone fifteen.

Check it by tapping down the side — a healthy tank rings hollow at the top and
dull at the bottom, and there is a clear line between the two. No line, no
bladder.
